Calm by design

This project's standing mandate is the opposite of an engagement-maximizing app: every mechanic below was chosen (and several were deliberately not built) to keep screen time something a family stays aware of, not something either the kid or the parent ends up fixated on checking. Each card names the mechanism, why it exists, and which screen shows it.

01

A calm signal, not exact numbers

The kid app's ongoing notification and home screen show one of three icons - comfortably under, approaching, or at the goal - never a running total. The app says outright why, right next to the icon.

Status screen
02

A pause, not a wall

Crossing halfway into an app's daily limit triggers a few quiet seconds before "Continue" enables - not a block, just a beat to notice the automatic reach for the app. Fires once per app per day.

Pause overlay
03

Always an off-ramp

A block screen suggests a concrete alternative activity (except at bedtime, where winding down is the point, not finding something else to do), and can be asked to open back up via "Request more time" - never a dead end.

Block overlay
04

Nothing here scores failure

Streaks only ever count up from zero. There is no "broken streak" state anywhere in the app - a day over the limit just silently isn't counted, never flagged or called out.

Status & Dashboard
05

Suggest, don't just impose

A kid can propose their own new limit with no passcode required - it's sent to a parent for approval, not applied directly. Limits are something negotiated, not only handed down.

Suggest a change
06

Detail stays with the parent - and says why

The weekly report's bar chart and by-app breakdown never reach the kid app. Both apps carry an explicit line explaining the asymmetry, framing the report as something to start a conversation with, not run silent surveillance.

Weekly report & Status
07

A short window, not an archive

The report only ever shows a rolling four weeks - enough to see a real trend, not something bounded to invite scrolling back through months of a kid's history.

Weekly report
08

The nudge points at the parent too

Opening the detailed report a 4th time in one day gets its own "Check in, not check up" prompt before it opens again - the same friction-pause idea from the kid side, turned back on the parent's own checking habit.

Weekly report
09

Walk the walk

Parental self-tracking reuses the exact same card, stats, and lock button a kid's profile gets - no separate, easier standard for the adult in the house.

Dashboard & Self tracking
10

Counting is opt-in - and named as a risk

Unlock and notification tracking are off by default, collect nothing until a parent turns them on, and count only (never notification content). If a parent shows a count on the kid's phone, it carries a note that watching counts can feed compulsive checking.

Child detail, Weekly report & Status
11

Honest answers, with sources

The help bot is a small offline retriever over a cited knowledge base, not an AI that might invent a health claim. It says so, cites WHO, the Canadian guidelines, the AAP and the UK CMOs, and says plainly when it has no answer.
